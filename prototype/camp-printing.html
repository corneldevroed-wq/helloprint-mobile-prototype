Promote Your Camp: Posters, Flyers and Signs That Get Bookings

Most camps fill through word of mouth and what parents see on the school run. Put the dates, the age groups and a QR code to your booking page in the places they pass every day.

schoolAt the school gate

An A-board at drop-off, a waterproof poster on the railings.

backpackIn every school bag

A DL flyer or a folded leaflet with dates, prices and the QR code.

flagOn the club railings

A banner facing the road from March, so every passing parent sees the dates.

storefrontIn the local shop and café

A poster in the window, postcards by the till for returning families.

Welcome Packs, Handbooks and Camp Paperwork

menu_bookThe parent welcome pack

What to bring, drop-off and pick-up times, the timetable and who to call. A stapled booklet or a trifold leaflet that stays on the fridge all week.

import_contactsThe leader handbook

Groups, rotas, safeguarding contacts and the rain plan. Wire binding lies flat on a clipboard.

event_noteOn the wall

The day's timetable, the group lists and the safeguarding statement, where everyone can see them: posters or aluminium signs.

edit_noteSign-in and sign-out

Carbon-copy sheets for collections, accidents and medicines, one copy for the parent: NCR sets.

groupsGroup and team cards

A card per group with the leader's name and the colour of the day: postcards or sticker sheets.

photo_libraryThe camp memory book

Photos and names from the week, for the families and next year's flyer: a perfect bound book.

Before camp week: the things organisers sort out first
  • Your child safeguarding statement goes up in a prominent place at the camp. A poster or an aluminium sign at the entrance does the job.
  • Leaders working with children need Garda vetting through your organisation.
  • First aid cover and insurance: check what your club, school or organisation already has.

General information, not legal advice. See the Children First Act 2015, section 11, and Tusla.
