Brief (Cornel, 2026-11-09): a combo of what we need and what Shopify does well. First a trusted, good feeling (a green strip on top). Then the actions the customer still has to do, on top and with the most attention: pay, upload files, or both. With more order lines the attention goes to the line and what it still needs. Shopify's order details with a Google map. And: always per order line, so 1 or more lines use the same layout. The upload is the PDP's drop field, nothing new.

Order of the page, every state: 1 green strip · 2 actions (only when there is something to do) · 3 your products, one card per line · 4 order details with the map · 5 people, proud line, Continue shopping.

Mobile · 6 states, one layout 1 line and 3 lines side by side. The actions block disappears when nothing is left; the product cards stay the same.
Desktop · the same in the checkout frame The 544 column holds the page; the card on the right only has the totals (the products are already the cards on the left). Two actions side by side.

Why this order

  • Green strip first: the very first thing is "it worked", in our header green, with the check. Trust before tasks.
  • Actions second, loudest: the only green-outlined, numbered blocks on the page, with the deadline in the title line. Money and files come in here: Pay shows the amount big, the logos and one button; Upload is the PDP drop field per product that still needs one.
  • Always per order line: 1 line is a list of one. Every card has the photo, the specs, the price, its own 4-step bar and date, and "Upload ↑" when it still waits for a file (it jumps to its action).
  • Order details + map: Shopify's calm grid (contact, payment, addresses, delivery, order number) with the map of the shipping address on top (Google Maps embed in the prototype).
  • All set: no actions block; the strip says "Everything's in, we'll take it from here".
