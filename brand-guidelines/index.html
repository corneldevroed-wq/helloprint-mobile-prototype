Brand Guidelines.

More than a brand.

HelloPrint is on a mission to make print easier, for everyone, everywhere. This brand guide is the key to how we express and strengthen our brand — across every channel, every touchpoint, every market.

In this guide we define the core of HelloPrint: our vision, values, tone of voice and visual identity. These guidelines ensure that every expression — from marketing campaigns to product visuals — is aligned with the unique character of HelloPrint.

By following these guidelines, we ensure our brand remains recognisable, professional, and inspiring wherever it appears.

03Voice & Tone.

Tone of voice.

HelloPrint's tone of voice is friendly, expert, and human. We speak plainly — no jargon, no watered-down copy, no corporate speak.

Our audience are entrepreneurs themselves. We respect their intelligence and their time. We lead with the benefit, keep it short, and always sound like a real person — not a marketing machine.

Friendly.
Expert.
Human.

How we speak.

Plain & direct

Lead with the message. One idea per section. Clarity always beats cleverness.

Confident, not arrogant

We know our product is great. We don't need to shout about it.

Warm & human

We're talking to real business owners. Sound like one.

Energetic when it counts

Playful in marketing. Calm and clear in transactional content.

Do & don’t.

✓  Use plain, everyday language

✓  Lead with the main benefit

✓  Be direct — say what you mean

✓  Keep paragraphs short

✗  Use print jargon without explaining it

✗  Sound salesy or overhyped

✗  Hide key info behind vague language

✗  Use handwritten font for prices or legal copy

✗  Write long, dense paragraphs

04Our Logo.

Primary logo.

Our wordmark is the primary expression of HelloPrint. Italic, bold, optimistic — it always leans forward.

Use it whenever space allows. The mark always sits on either dark green or cream. No outlines, no drop shadows, no gradients.

Clear space & sizing.

Clear space

Always leave at least the height of the "H" of clear space around the logo on all sides. Nothing competes with the mark.

Minimum size

Never smaller than 18 px high on screen or 10 mm in print. Legibility first.

On every background.

Logo do & don’t.

✓ Do

Keep it italic, bold, and tight

Use cream on dark, dark on cream

Pair with generous clear space

Scale uniformly

✗ Don't

Don't outline, recolour, or stylise

Don't stretch, skew, or rotate

Don't place on low-contrast photos

Don't add drop shadows or effects

Don't re-typeset the wordmark

Construction.

The wordmark is set in custom italics, kerned by hand. The slope is 12°, the cap-height to x-height ratio is 1.18, and the stroke contrast is held to a single weight.

Never re-typeset. Always use the master artwork.

Monogram & favicon.

When to use the H

Where the wordmark won't fit: app icons, favicons, social avatars, social ad endcards.

Always italic

The H carries the same slope as the wordmark. Cream on green or green on cream — never inverted.

08Graphic Elements.

Building blocks.

Three shapes, three jobs. Use them sparingly — composition first, decoration never.

Green Block

Anchors a headline or USP. Always full-bleed within its container.

Outlined Card

Groups information. 10 px corners, 1 px light grey line; on desktop a soft shadow on hover only.

Image Slot

Square or rectangular, with the card corners (10 px) — let the photography speak.

Iconography.

Google Material Symbols Rounded, line (weight 400, 24 px), no fill. Dark green — never coloured-in.

Use icons to clarify, not decorate. One icon per idea, never two side-by-side.

Patterns.

Three repeating patterns, derived from the wordmark and the box. Use for endpapers, wrapping, mailers, internal swag.

Never behind body copy. Never as a presentation slide background.

Motion.

Movement is energetic, never decorative. Three principles keep motion on-brand.

Fast

280 ms default. Anything longer and it feels slow.

Out

Ease-out curves. Things land softly, never bounce.

Up

Type enters from below by 12 px. Mirrors how a sheet drops from a press.
